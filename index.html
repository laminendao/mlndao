Short bio

Since October 2025, I have been a Temporary Teaching and Research Assistant (ATER) at the Conservatoire National des Arts et Métiers (CNAM) in Paris. I received my PhD in Applied Mathematics from CNAM in December 2025, and I am qualified for Maître de Conférences positions (CNU sections 26 and 27).

My doctoral research, carried out at CESI LINEACT and the CEDRIC laboratory, focused on explainable artificial intelligence for multivariate, multi-sensor time series, with applications to Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction in predictive maintenance. I worked under the supervision of Ndèye Niang (CNAM), Genane Youness (CESI LINEACT) and Gilbert Saporta (CNAM).

My research is about how preprocessing, model complexity and feature structure affect what post-hoc explanations (SHAP, LIME, L2X) actually reveal, and about interpretable dimensionality reduction methods, such as divisive feature clustering, that make deep models easier to read.

Keywords: explainable AI, multivariate time series, predictive maintenance, remaining useful life, feature clustering, dimensionality reduction, unsupervised and self-supervised representation learning, multi-view clustering.

Research

Machine learning for multivariate, multi-sensor data from industrial systems, with a focus on making models interpretable and robust.

Evaluating XAI methods

Quantitative evaluation of post-hoc explanations (SHAP, LIME, L2X) and of how preprocessing and model complexity change them.

Interpretable dimension reduction

Divisive feature clustering and clustering of variables around latent components, used to build readable inputs for RUL models.

Predictive maintenance

RUL prediction from multi-sensor time series (C-MAPSS turbofans, PRONOSTIA / FEMTO-ST bearings) with deep models such as LSTMs.

Heterogeneous data

Multi-block and multi-view clustering, consensus clustering and missing data.
